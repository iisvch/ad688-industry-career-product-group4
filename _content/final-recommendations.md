## Where to Focus

For students and early-career applicants pursuing Data Analyst roles in securities, investments and related financial activities, the clearest priorities are:

- **Strengthen SQL, Python and business-intelligence skills.**
- **Target roles that match your experience.**
- **Use salary benchmarks to compare opportunities.**
- **Confirm location and remote requirements in each posting.**

These recommendations combine the [data preparation](data_preparation.qmd), [market baseline](market_baseline.qmd), [skill-gap analysis](skill_gap_analysis.qmd) and [salary analysis](ml_methods.qmd). The evidence covers **140 postings from May–September 2024**, representing **102 distinct employer/title/description profiles**. It is a historical snapshot, not a list of current vacancies.

## 1. Build the Skills Employers Request

![Skill demand across posting rows and distinct posting profiles](figures/final_skill_demand.png)

After counting repeated descriptions once, the leading skills among the eight technical groups examined are:

| Skill | Share of distinct profiles | Practical evidence to build |
|---|---:|---|
| SQL | 67.6% | Queries using joins, aggregation and window functions to answer business questions. |
| Python | 51.0% | A reproducible notebook that cleans data, analyzes patterns and explains the findings. |
| Power BI / Tableau | 50.0% | A dashboard with useful filters, clear measures and a short business explanation. |

Percentages use **102 distinct profiles**. The BI group includes either Power BI or Tableau; the two bars in the chart show how repeated postings affect demand counts.

The original skill-gap analysis also found **Data Analysis in 69.3%** and **Communication in 60.7%** of the 140 postings. Show that you can explain what the numbers mean and recommend a practical action, alongside demonstrating the tools.

For Group 4, the self-assessment identifies strengths in **Excel, visualization, statistics and business analysis**, with lower average ratings in **SQL and Python (2.33/5)**. The development priorities remain:

- **Taiqi:** SQL and Python first; continue improving BI skills.
- **Joseph and Igor:** SQL, Python and BI first.
- Build on existing strengths to produce clear, business-focused work. These ratings are self-assessments, not verified proficiency scores.

## 2. Target Roles That Fit Your Experience

![Reported minimum experience requirements for 112 of the 140 postings](figures/revised/experience_requirements.png)

- **26 postings, representing 25 profiles, require 0–2 years** of minimum experience. Start by reviewing these roles and relevant junior or associate titles.
- **79 postings require 3–5 years**. Read the responsibilities carefully before deciding whether your previous work meets the requirement.
- **28 postings omit minimum experience**. Check the description; missing information does not mean no experience is required.

Match your application to the actual responsibilities and required skills, rather than relying on the title alone.

## 3. Use Salary as a Benchmark

![Annual salary midpoints across postings and distinct posting profiles](figures/ml_salary_distribution.png)

- Salary is available for **86 postings**, representing **48 distinct profiles**.
- The distinct-profile median annual midpoint is approximately **$92,300**.
- The middle 50% of profile midpoints falls roughly between **$72,000 and $100,000**.
- This describes roles with different experience levels; it is not an entry-level salary expectation or an individual offer range.

Compare each employer's advertised range with similar roles, experience requirements and location. The best salary model still has an average absolute error of about **$18,300**, so the descriptive benchmark is more useful than treating a predicted number as a precise offer expectation.

## 4. Search Across Employers and Work Arrangements

![States with the most distinct posting profiles in the sample](figures/final_state_opportunities.png)

- **Massachusetts, North Carolina and Pennsylvania** lead the sample by distinct profiles. Use them as starting points for employer research, alongside locations that fit your circumstances.
- **Lincoln Financial Group, Vanguard and Fidelity Investments** are prominent in the market baseline. Use these names to explore relevant roles, while also considering other employers.
- **54 postings are classified as remote**, but **71 have an unknown arrangement**. Verify attendance and location requirements directly in the posting.
- A job advertised in several cities may be one opening. Compare descriptions and requirements before treating every listing as a separate opportunity.

These patterns show where the historical sample advertised roles; they do not establish which employers are hiring today or which location offers the best cost-adjusted salary.

## A Practical Next Step

Build **one finance-related portfolio project** that combines the three priority tools:

1. Use **SQL** to organize the data and calculate meaningful business measures.
2. Use **Python** to clean, analyze and visualize the results.
3. Use **Power BI or Tableau** to present a dashboard and explain the decisions it supports.
4. Add the project to your CV and tailor the application to the skills and experience in each target role.

For the career-readiness prototype, put these decisions first: show skill matches, learning priorities, experience fit and salary benchmarks with sample sizes. The study supports practical preparation and a more focused search; it does not guarantee a job offer or a salary increase.

## Limitations of These Recommendations

- The small 2024 sample and uneven regional coverage do not support firm conclusions about the full or current labor market.
- Missing salary, experience and remote information can bias comparisons; repeated advertising can inflate apparent opportunity counts.
- Team skill ratings are self-assessments, and the salary model remains too uncertain for precise personal predictions.
- The prototype provides preparation and search guidance. It does not yet track live vacancies, verify readiness, or measure hiring outcomes.
