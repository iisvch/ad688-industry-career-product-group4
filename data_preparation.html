Data Preparation

Step 2 — Data Preparation and Career-Market Scope

Step 2 Objective

This step prepares the career-market data used to evaluate opportunities for Data Analyst careers within the selected financial-services industry.

The analysis is anchored to:

  • Career pathway: Data Analyst / Data Analytics
  • NAICS: 523
  • Industry: Securities, Commodity Contracts, and Other Financial Investments and Related Activities

The objective is to create a cleaned, reproducible dataset that can be used to evaluate job availability, compensation, experience requirements, work arrangements, geography, employers, and skills.

Data Source

The analysis uses the professor-approved Lightcast job-postings dataset provided through the team’s Google Drive dataset.

The dataset contains job-posting information including job titles, employers, locations, salary information, experience requirements, education, remote-work classifications, skills, occupation classifications, and NAICS industry classifications.

Industry Filter

The analysis is restricted to NAICS 523, the three-digit NAICS subsector:

Securities, Commodity Contracts, and Other Financial Investments and Related Activities

The Lightcast dataset contains 942 job postings classified under NAICS 523.

Using the three-digit NAICS3 field ensures that the analysis covers the full selected NAICS subsector rather than treating 523000 as a separate six-digit industry code.

Career Filter

The career pathway is Data Analyst / Data Analytics.

A title-based filter was used to identify postings containing Data Analyst terminology. Clearly unrelated specialized categories were excluded when the title indicated a maintenance, cybersecurity, academic, internship, platform-services, CV-22, or SC2 role.

The resulting core career-market dataset contains 140 postings.

This core dataset is used as the baseline for Step 2. Closely related analytics roles may be incorporated into the broader Step 3 skill-gap analysis where appropriate.

Cleaning Decisions

Duplicate Records

Postings were checked using the Lightcast ID field. No duplicate IDs were removed from the 140-posting analytical dataset.

Salary

Salary fields were converted to numeric values where possible. Hourly and monthly amounts were annualized during the earlier preparation, so the stored endpoints are used without another conversion. The original pay-period label can remain unchanged.

Missing salary values were retained as missing rather than converted to zero.

A salary midpoint was calculated when both the lower and upper salary values were available:

\[ Salary\ Midpoint = \frac{Salary\ From + Salary\ To}{2} \]

There were 86 postings with a usable salary midpoint.

Across the 86 posting rows, the observed annual salary midpoint ranged from $24,960 to $160,000, with a median of $93,850. After counting repeated employer/title/description profiles once, the median across 48 salary profiles is approximately $92,300.

Because salary information is not available for every posting, these figures describe the subset of postings reporting usable salary ranges.

Experience

Minimum and maximum experience requirements were retained separately.

Negative or invalid experience values were treated as missing.

A minimum experience requirement was available for 112 of the 140 postings.

Among postings with reported minimum experience, the median requirement was 5 years, with an average of approximately 4.1 years.

Maximum experience was substantially less complete and was therefore not imputed.

Remote Work

Remote-work classifications were retained from the Lightcast data.

The cleaned categories are:

  • Remote
  • Hybrid Remote
  • Not Remote
  • Unknown

Of the 140 postings:

  • 54 were classified as Remote
  • 12 were classified as Hybrid Remote
  • 3 were classified as Not Remote
  • 71 had an unknown classification

Unknown values were retained because an unavailable classification does not necessarily mean that the position is onsite.

Location

City and state information were retained for geographic analysis.

No postings were missing the cleaned city or state fields in the analytical dataset.

The location data were used to identify the leading hiring states and locations represented in the sample.

Education and Skills

Education and skill fields were retained from the Lightcast dataset.

Missing values were not converted into assumptions about required education or skills.

These fields provide the foundation for the later skill-gap analysis.

Final Analytical Dataset

The cleaned Step 2 dataset contains:

  • 140 job postings
  • 52 employers
  • 62 distinct job titles
  • 36 original analytical variables
  • Additional cleaned variables for remote status, location, salary midpoint, industry scope, and career scope

The cleaned dataset is stored as:

data/career_market_panel.csv

The corresponding variable documentation is stored as:

data/data_dictionary.csv

Step 2 Limitations

Several data-quality limitations should be considered when interpreting the results.

First, salary information is unavailable for 54 of the 140 postings.

Second, minimum experience is unavailable for 28 postings, while maximum experience is unavailable for most postings.

Third, 71 postings do not have a classified remote-work arrangement.

Finally, the dataset represents job postings rather than unique underlying job openings or hiring demand. Multiple postings may represent similar positions or repeated recruiting activity.

These limitations are documented rather than addressed through aggressive imputation so that the baseline remains transparent and reproducible.
